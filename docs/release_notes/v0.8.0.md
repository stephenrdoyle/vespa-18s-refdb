# vespa18s v0.8.0: curation from real samples, and how firm each species call is

97,739 records (46 added, 3 removed, 31 renamed or resolved differently). 49,040 resolve to species; 8,983 are helminth records. This release follows the first runs of the VESPA public data and a VESPA-primer nanopore flow cell against v0.7.0.

## What's new

- **How firmly each species is named.** Each resolution table has a new `margin` column: for records that resolve to species, the edits to the nearest record of another species, over that table's view (whole insert, or the two Illumina windows). About 30% of species-level records rest on 1–2 edits. A read from a lineage the database lacks, a base or two away, would be named just as confidently, so a pipeline can treat those calls with care. Example: *Trichinella nelsoni* resolves at 2x301 by a single base.
- **What the taxonomy columns mean.** The nine columns are PR2's positional levels, not Linnaean ranks: in animals and most fungi `class` holds the phylum (Nematoda, Ascomycota), `order` a class and `family` an order or class. The new `rank_names.tsv` gives the mapping for the major clades, so "named to family" can be read correctly.
- ***Blastocystis*.** All 100 NCBI genome assemblies are now scanned (only 9 were before), and PubMLST's 87 curated, subtype-labelled 18S alleles are added as subtype references. PubMLST agreed with every subtype the release had already assigned (82 of 82). 596 of 658 *Blastocystis* records are now named to subtype (v0.7.0: 581 of 653), including ST14/ST24 cases that were previously left unassigned.
- **Gut protists that were missing.** A targeted GenBank layer adds trichomonads (*Tetratrichomonas*, *Trichomitus*, *Hypotrichomonas*), diplomonads, archamoebae and gut ciliates (*Buxtonella* and others). It also adds sequences that BLAST found for unmatched VESPA clusters: a primate *Buxtonella*-like ciliate those reads now match at 98% (was 93%), and closer *Paramicrosporidium* and Hexamitinae references.
- ***Balantidium coli* under one name.** PR2 calls it *Neobalantidium*; NCBI calls it *Balantioides*. It's now *Balantioides coli* throughout, so reads that matched both names are now named to species.
- **More contaminants and mislabels fixed.** Two *Angulamoeba* strains and a cryptophyte that PR2 had filed as a red alga and a ciliate are relabelled. A "*Trachelomonas* chloroplast" that is bacterial 16S, and two diatom transcriptome contigs carrying kinetoplastid 18S, are dropped. Each change is in `curation/overrides.tsv` with its BLAST evidence.
- **`summary.txt`** now also shows primer fit by domain (all bacterial records sit at the 2-edit limit with 3′-end mismatches) and the clades V4 splits least, such as Syndiniales and many fungi. A call that stops in one of those clades means "genus unknowable from V4", not "badly classified".

## Still open

VESPA's *Entamoeba*, *Iodamoeba* and *Endolimax* reads, and the human and primate gut flagellates, have no closer sequence in GenBank, PubMLST, genome assemblies or ENA reads. Full-length V4 from the samples themselves would fill these gaps.

## Format

The changes are additive: a trailing `margin` column in `resolution_*.tsv`, a new `rank_names.tsv`, and new manifest counts (`primer_fit_by_domain`, `species_margins`). A reader that selects columns by name is unaffected.

## Download and verify

```bash
V=v0.8.0
curl -LO https://github.com/stephenrdoyle/vespa-18s-refdb/releases/download/$V/vespa18s_$V.tar.gz
shasum -a 256 vespa18s_$V.tar.gz
# f9e0c4b31a379c14171079d7f7341fd02a7e943afbe2db3c03a8631d5b164145
```

Check each file against `releases/vespa18s_v0.8.0/manifest.json`, as the README describes. Full details are in `docs/DESIGN.md` §4.7, §4.8, §5.1 and §6.8.
