# Response to the v0.4.0 feedback, in v0.5.0

To `v0.4.0_feedback.md` (from nf-helminth-amplicon's run on VESPA's
public data). Thank you - every point was checked against the release.

## 1a. Mixed-kingdom records

General rules in `bin/collapse.py`, applied before conflicts are judged,
rather than hand edits, so they also catch the cases no read has hit yet:

- **Prokaryote label on a eukaryote-length insert (>= 450bp).** A
  bacterial 16S amplifies with these primers to about 370bp; these are
  host 18S inside bacterial assemblies. 128 members dropped; 24 records
  had nothing else and are gone - among them `V4_ef59fbd391ef`, the
  human 18S labelled *Arthrobacter*. Also resolves `V4_3f106295884f`
  (*Papilio*), `V4_a563ba48d877` (*Ceratitis*), `V4_d421519d6cd7`
  (*Linum*), `V4_9fb9f04077cb` (*Arabidopsis*), `V4_92cc115de72e`
  (*Prunus*) and `V4_5c63ee87f161` (Embryophyceae).
- **Organelle label beside a nuclear one** (`:mito`, `:plas`, `:apic`,
  `:chro`; not `:nucl` - a nucleomorph's 18S is an 18S): 382 members.
  Resolves `V4_2899f470c502` (*Colpoda steinii*) and `V4_a9ab4ce9d60e`.
- **A directly sequenced isolate outranks WGS/TSA contigs** that
  conflict with it. Resolves `V4_9b8b7df9a086` (*Neobodo*) and
  `V4_ee9297e5154b` (*Procryptobia*).
- **`curation/overrides.tsv`** for the judgements no rule can make, each
  with its evidence: `V4_627358604901` keeps *Hartmannella*, and
  `V4_54f09b637b29` keeps *Morchella*, as you suggested.
- `V4_5b7f23fcd989` resolves to *Malus* by majority once its bacterial
  member is dropped.

Left at a higher rank on purpose, the evidence being genuinely split:
`V4_f5fe7cb39d52` (*Malus* WGS contigs against *Saccharomyces* strain
assemblies), `V4_31aee29f4dba` (two *Halophytophthora* isolates against
three fungal records), `V4_d9e47beca719` (*Alphamonas* against
*Chlamydomonas*). Dropped members stay in `members.tsv` with role
`dropped` and the reason in a new `note` column.

The suggested systematic neighbour check (>= 99%-identical records
disagreeing at supergroup) is not built yet; the prokaryote-length rule
catches the commonest form of it.

## 1b. Host name as species

`V4_a048af0a7d80` is now *Leidyana* sp. A species name whose genus is
used in another class becomes Genus_sp. (3 further cases, among them
*Myotis davidii*, a bat, on a *Toxoplasma* record from the bat's genome).
A broader name test was tried and rejected: the other apicomplexan
records whose species does not start with their genus all name genuine
apicomplexan genera that PR2 files under a clade (fish *Eimeria* under
*Goussia*, *Besnoitia* under *Toxoplasma*) - checked against NCBI
taxonomy; only *Trachyphloeus* is a host, recorded as an override.

## 2. Taxa to add

Not yet. The candidates will be used as queries to find close

**Update, after v0.6.0 (2026-10-28): the amoebae.** The 31 candidates
nearest *Entamoeba*, *Iodamoeba* and *Endolimax* (29 *Entamoeba*, one
each of the others; no *Enteromonas* candidate reached the list) went to
NCBI BLAST (blastn megablast, core_nt). Summary in
`results/candidates/amoebae_blast_summary.tsv`, full result in
`amoebae_blast.xml.gz`.

- **GenBank holds nothing closer than the release already does.** For
  every candidate, the best named hit is a record the release holds (30)
  or one already fetched for the GenBank layer (1, cand013, identical
  over V4 to records in the release). Named sequences the database does
  not hold are all short partials covering about one read window
  (identity 0.25-0.64 over the joined query); uncultured and
  environmental records are no closer.
- **So the gap is real:** the *E. hartmanni*-like variants (93-97% to
  the nearest record), the divergent *E. coli*-like ones and the
  *Iodamoeba* candidate have not been deposited anywhere. Their reads
  are named to the right genus, not misassigned, only less deep.
- **The candidates are not added themselves.** Each is 250bp + 10 N +
  200bp, not a whole insert: as a record it would serve the Illumina
  windows only, and nanopore reads not at all. Full-length sequences of
  these lineages - nanopore of a few high-count samples, or whole
  inserts assembled from long reads - would fill the gap properly.
- **For the pipeline:** for three candidates BLAST finds a release
  record markedly closer than the identity in the feedback: cand057
  0.903 -> 0.947 and cand069 0.901 -> 0.944 (both FR686408, *E. coli*,
  `V4_bc024fe3339c`), cand067 0.877 -> 0.936 (JN635745, *Iodamoeba*,
  `V4_b588224fdbc2`). Both records are kept and resolve as expected, so
  the pipeline's identity measure is likely stricter than BLAST's -
  perhaps counting end gaps or the N spacer - and worth a look.

**Onchocerca:** its V4 is 1 edit from *Loa loa* and 2 from
*Dirofilaria repens*, so VESPA's *Onchocerca* calls may well be
near-neighbour calls. The *O. volvulus* member spans the whole insert.
*D. immitis* is the outlier: about 30 edits from all three. The
*D. immitis* WSI 2.2 assembly carries three V4 copies (1-2bp apart),
identical or near-identical to the existing records; they are now local

## 3. Bacterial 16S

Confirmed from the release's own primer-fit table: in v0.7.0 all 2,141
bacterial and archaeal records are `near_3prime` - mismatched in a
primer's last five bases, at exactly the 2-edit limit of the in-silico
PCR - while 81% of eukaryote records fit both primers exactly. Gut
bacteria a further edit away never enter the database, as you found.

**Decision: count only, for now.** The release stays a eukaryote
database with the borderline prokaryote records it already has; the
pipeline reports bacterial reads as an off-target fraction. A prokaryote
layer (SILVA trimmed to 515-926, marked off-target so its looser
threshold cannot leak into eukaryote resolution) remains the route if
bacteria ever need naming.

The suggested primer-fit summary by domain goes into `summary.txt` from
the next release, so the bacterial exposure is visible per release.

### 5d. The primate samples

- ***Physaloptera*.** The release has 8 records (*P. turgida*, *alata*,
  *sibirica*, *thalacomys*, *apivori* and three *P.* sp.). The "nearest
  *Howardula*" sequences we have (cand042, cand064, from the human
  samples) are not *Physaloptera*: over their two windows they are 16-17
  edits (~3.5%) from *Howardula* and *Deladenus* - tylenchids, insect
  parasites and fungal feeders, likely dietary - and ~89 from every
  *Physaloptera* record. If the primate cluster is the same, it is not
  *Physaloptera* either. A primate *Physaloptera* read should land
  within a few percent of one of the 8 records; consensus sequences of
  the primate cluster, and of reads VESPA called *Physaloptera*, would
  let us test that.
- **Trichostome ciliates.** More than 2 records: *Balantioides coli*
  (14, GenBank), *Neobalantidium coli* (11, PR2), *Buxtonella sulcata*
  (6), *Troglodytella abrassarti* (7, the chimpanzee ciliate),
  *Isotricha* (25) and *Balantidium* (6). One real problem: *B. coli* is
  here under two genus names - NCBI's *Balantioides* and PR2's
  *Neobalantidium*, synonyms - so a read tying across both stops above
  genus. Now harmonised under NCBI's *Balantioides* (39 relabel lines in
  `curation/overrides.tsv`, next release): the 26 records become 30
  *Balantioides coli* records, 4 of them records that had stopped above
  genus. Reads 93% to *Neobalantidium* are too
  far for any of these; primate trichostome sequences (or probes from
  the samples) are needed.
- **Commensal amoebae and flagellates:** as in 5c; agreed that
  primate-host isolates are what is missing.
- ***Trichinella nelsoni*.** Two records (the WBPS genome and AY851261),
  2-3 edits from the other *Trichinella* and >= 78 edits (~14%) from
  every other genus, including the capillariids (14 *Capillaria*, 7
  *Aonchotheca*, 10 *Eucoleus*, 5 *Pearsonema*) and 32 *Trichuris*.
  The labels look right. So the question is the reads' distance: if they
  are within a few edits, it is *Trichinella* (a finding); if they are
  far, it is a capillarid or trichurid missing from the database that
  borrowed the nearest name - the leave-one-out simulation shows the

  distance of those reads to `V4_1b75ba882512` / `V4_97eac3c4dd50`?
- **Primate *Entamoeba*:** the release has *E. polecki* (17 records),

  2,062 *Entamoeba* 18S records fetched from GenBank. For the human

### 5e. *Trichinella*, and the "*Howardula*" cluster

Agreed on both; thank you for the raw-read checks.

- ***T. nelsoni* is resolvable, but by a single base.** Read whole
  (nanopore) and through 2x301 windows, `V4_97eac3c4dd50` (AY851261)
  resolves to species; at 2x150 only to genus. But over the 2x301
  windows it is **1 edit** from the record that *T. britovi*, *T.
  murrelli* and *T. nativa* share, and 1 from the one that also holds
  *T. patagoniensis* and genotypes T6, T8 and T9. It is 2 edits (0 in
  the windows) from the WBPS *T. nelsoni* genome's own copy. So the
  genus call is robust and the species name rests on one position: a
  *Trichinella* lineage the release lacks, one base away, would be
  named *T. nelsoni* just as confidently.
- **The release already carries 12 *Trichinella* species and
  genotypes**, mostly from the WBPS genomes (*britovi*, *murrelli*, *nativa*,
  *nelsoni*, *papuae*, *patagoniensis*, *pseudospiralis*, *spiralis*,
  *zimbabwensis*, T6, T8, T9); most share V4 and sit in 6 genus-level
  records. Our GenBank download adds no further *Trichinella* species.
- **Proposal:** the resolution tables say *whether* a record resolves,
  not *how firmly*. Adding the edits to the nearest record of another
  species, over the same windows, would let the pipeline flag calls
  like this one ("species, by 1 edit"). It would be a new column in
  each `resolution_*.tsv`.
- ***Howardula* / *Physaloptera*:** as you found - the cluster is a
  tylenchid (our BLAST of the human-sample probes said the same: 3.5%
  from *Howardula* and *Deladenus*, ~89 edits from any *Physaloptera*),
  and *Physaloptera* is present but below the floor.

### 5f. Chimeras

Noted. No local sequences will come from candidates that were not

### 5g. Fungi

- **PR2's ranks are positional, not Linnaean - for animals too.** The
  nine columns are PR2's nine levels, whatever rank the name holds. For
  fungi `class` holds the phylum, as you say; for animals it does the
  same: `class` is Nematoda or Platyhelminthes (phyla), `order` is
  Chromadorea (a class), `family` is Rhabditida (an order). So per-rank
  summaries overstate depth for helminths as well as fungi. Proposal:
  keep PR2's columns (comparability), state this in the README and
  release notes, and ship a small `rank_names.tsv` giving, for the major
  clades, the Linnaean rank each column holds - which the pipeline can
  use for its summaries and floors.
- **Clades V4 cannot split:** agreed. A summary.txt section listing the
  large clades whose records mostly stop at or above class (as read by
  their resolution tables) would make "Eurotiomycetes" read as "a mould,
  genus unknowable".

The neighbour check behind the *Vigna* call (`docs/DESIGN.md` §5.1)
also dropped 31 contaminated members and relabelled 4 in v0.7.0, with
BLAST evidence for each - among them a whole "*Eimeria*" study that was
yeast, birch and mushroom 18S - and five more since, for the next
