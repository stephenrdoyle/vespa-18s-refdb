# Notes for amphid, with vespa18s v0.8.0

From vespa-18s-refdb, 2026-10-29. This collects, in one place, what the pipeline side should know from the work since v0.7.0. Background: `docs/feedback/v0.4.0_response.md` (our answers to your feedback), `docs/feedback/amphid_call_depth_recommendation.md`, and `docs/DESIGN.md` §4.7, §4.8, §5.1 and §6.8.

## 1. New in the release format: please read these

All three are additive. A reader that selects columns by name is unaffected.

1. **`margin` in each `resolution_*.tsv`.** This is a new trailing column. For records that resolve to species, it gives the edits to the nearest record of another species, over that table's view: the whole insert, or the two windows. It's blank where a record doesn't reach species, and blank on a species row when no other species is near (a large margin). About 30% of species-level records rest on 1–2 edits (summary.txt has the counts). Suggested use: report it with the call, for example "*Trichinella nelsoni*, by 1 edit", and treat 1–2 as fragile.
2. **`rank_names.tsv`.** The nine taxonomy columns are PR2's positional levels, not Linnaean ranks. In animals and most fungi, `class` holds the phylum (Nematoda, Ascomycota), `order` a class or subphylum, and `family` an order, class or subclass. Per-rank summaries ("named to family or better") and a family-level identity floor currently treat that column as a family; for helminths and fungi it's deeper in the tree than that. The table gives the mapping by clade.
3. **Manifest:** `primer_fit_by_domain` and `species_margins` counts. These are informational.

## 2. Recommendations from our analysis

4. **Cap call depth by distance to the best hit.** `bin/simulate_leave_one_out.py` removed each of about 4,000 helminth and apicomplexan species in turn. With the species missing and its genus present, 27–46% of reads were named as a sister species (exact or 2x150 reads). Calibrated against the same reads with the species present, the suggested starting values are: **species only within 1.5% (Illumina) or 3% (nanopore) of the best hit; genus within 5%.** At those values, 96% of correct species calls are kept and 65–72% of wrong ones become genus or above. Details and caveats are in `amphid_call_depth_recommendation.md`. The margin (item 1) and the cap complement each other: the margin says how fragile a record is, and the cap says how far the read is from it.
5. **Ties across genera.** Some VESPA clusters are within 3–4% of one genus's records but are reported only to family. *Enteromonas*-like reads are 96.7% to an *Enteromonas* record but reported as Hexamitidae, and *Chilomastix*-like reads are 96.9% but reported as Retortamonadidae. It looks as though near-equal hits from several genera pull the LCA up. It's worth checking how the tie tolerance treats hits that differ by an edit or two.
6. **Labels from disagreeing windows usually mean chimeras or artefacts, not gaps.** We BLASTed the largest unmatched clusters. Those labelled "Eukaryota, unresolved below domain (94% to X)" or similar were yeast or *Blastocystis* fragments with mismatched windows. One of them was the apparent "*Viannaia*" nematode, which isn't a nematode at all. Reporting them as likely chimeras would stop them reading as missing organisms.
7. **The identity measure looks stricter than BLAST's.** For three candidates, BLAST finds a record already in the release markedly closer than the identity amphid reported: cand057 0.903 → 0.947 and cand069 0.901 → 0.944 (both FR686408, *E. coli*), and cand067 0.877 → 0.936 (JN635745, *Iodamoeba*). It's possibly counting end gaps or the N spacer between windows.
8. **Low-identity clusters (17–25%)** from the known one-window alignment problem make up much of the apparent gregarine, *Blastocystis* and fungal gaps. The fix you mentioned would remove them from the gap picture.

## 3. Requests

9. **The *T. nelsoni* reads.** Your raw-read check shows an exact match to `V4_97eac3c4dd50`. The margin is 1 edit at 2x301, so the species name rests on one base. A check outside the pipeline before relying on the species would be prudent.
10. **Candidates re-derived with chimeras excluded,** including any near *Chilomastix*, *Enteromonas*, *Giardia* and *Pentatrichomonas*, none of which the current 86 covers. Also primate-sample candidates for *Entamoeba* and the trichostome ciliates.
11. **Full-length V4 for the protist gaps.** For *Entamoeba* (human *E. hartmanni*- and *E. coli*-like, and the primate species), *Iodamoeba* and *Endolimax*, nothing closer exists in any public source we checked: GenBank and BLAST, PubMLST, genome assemblies, and ENA reads. The one ENA "*E. hartmanni*" genome run is a mislabelled bacterium. Nanopore amplicons from a few high-count VESPA samples are the realistic route; consensus sequences would go in as local sequences.

## 4. Decisions

12. **Bacteria:** counted only, for now. The release stays a eukaryote database with the borderline prokaryote records it has. All 2,141 are at the 2-edit primer limit with 3′-end mismatches (see primer fit by domain).
13. **Partial references:** not admitted yet. About 8,200 named species exist only as partial 18S. Only 49 of them are near-complete (10 bp short or less) and could enter as flagged records. A one-window tier, which would add about 570 helminth species for Illumina, would need amphid to handle records that cover only one window. That's a design question for both sides (`docs/DESIGN.md` §8).

## 5. What v0.8.0 changes for VESPA-type data

- ***Blastocystis*:** 596 of 658 records are now named to subtype (v0.7.0: 581). PubMLST's curated alleles agreed with every earlier assignment, and the ST14/ST24 ambiguities are resolved.
- ***Balantidium coli*:** now one name, *Balantioides coli*. Reads that tied across PR2's *Neobalantidium* and NCBI's *Balantioides* now reach species.
- **Primate gut ciliate:** a *Buxtonella*-like reference that your 93% "*Neobalantidium*" cluster matches at 98%.
- **Gut trichomonads, diplomonads and archamoebae** added from GenBank. They don't close the VESPA gaps, but they add coverage for livestock and birds.
