# Feedback for vespa-18s-refdb, from releases v0.4.0 and v0.7.0 in use

From nf-helminth-amplicon's `--reference_database` mode run against VESPA's public data (BioProject PRJNA944233: 12 human and 40 non-human primate faecal samples, MiSeq 2x301), and against a VESPA-primer nanopore flow cell (FBH77883, 50 wells, section 3). Branch `claude/reference-database-mode-9016ac` (now `nf-amphid-pipeline`), benchmark `tests/bench_vespa_public.py`, 2026-10-28. Section 5 follows up on v0.7.0 (2026-10-29).

Every record below was hit by real reads. Each problem made those reads stop at a shallower rank than they should, or at no rank at all.

Pipeline side, for information: nf-helminth-amplicon now leaves records with no taxonomy out of the LCA rather than letting them veto it. Records with a domain only still collapse the LCA, which is correct while the label is unknown.

## 2. Taxa to add

These are well-supported sequences in VESPA's samples that could be named only above genus, because the release has no record close enough. `candidate_local_sequences.fasta` beside this file holds 86 representative consensus sequences: each found in ≥2 samples with ≥200 read pairs, and 85–99% to its nearest record. Each is the first 250 and last 200 bp of the insert, joined by ten Ns. That makes them evidence of what's missing and probes to find matching full-length GenBank sequences, not full-length references themselves.

## 3. Bacterial 16S: amplified in practice, barely in the release

Evidence: a VESPA-primer nanopore flow cell (FBH77883, run 2026-10-22, 50 wells, 659k reads; human stool-like columns plus worm or egg DNA columns), analysed with nf-helminth-amplicon's database mode against v0.4.0.

**The primers amplify gut bacteria heavily.** In the stool-like wells, 66–72% of classified reads were Bacteria. A further ~20% of reads, mostly 400–500 bp, were at first not classified at all (see below). VESPA's published human samples had ≤2.5% bacteria, so this is likely a property of the PCR conditions (here 50 °C annealing) as much as of the primers.

**Primer distances, reads and release:**

| | forward primer edits (0 / 1 / 2 / 3 / 4+) | reverse primer edits (0 / 1 / 2 / 3 / 4+) |
|---|---|---|
| reads ≥520 bp (18S-sized), n = 2,890 | 80% / 9 / 6 / 2 / 4 | 74% / 12 / 8 / 2 / 3 |
| reads <520 bp (16S-sized), n = 7,110 | 3% / 21 / 49 / 17 / 10 | 76% / 12 / 6 / 2 / 4 |
| release: Eukaryota records (93,986) | 88% / 11 / 2 / – / – | 91% / 7 / 1 / – / – |
| release: Bacteria + Archaea records (2,166) | 1% / 0 / 99 / – / – | 1% / 1 / 98 / – / – |

(Read edits include nanopore sequencing errors, ~2% at HAC, which is why even 18S reads aren't all exact.)

**Why:**
- **The forward primer** `AGCAGCCGCGGTAATTCC` sits on the universal "515F" region, which bacterial 16S shares (`…AGCAGCCGCGGTAATAC`). The difference is 2–3 bases at the 3′ end. That 3′ end is the only thing keeping bacteria out.
- **The reverse primer** `TCCGTCAATTYCTTNAASTTTC` closely resembles the universal bacterial 907R/926R primer (`CCGTCAATTCMTTTRAGTTT`). Its degenerate positions fit bacteria about as well as eukaryotes, so it adds no selectivity.
- The two together give a ~410 bp product from 16S (515–926), which matches the short reads.

(The sequence comparisons here are by eye against the standard primer sequences, not a full alignment; worth confirming in the repo's own primer-fit code.)

**What this means for the release:**
1. **Bacterial coverage is limited to borderline matches.** The in-silico PCR keeps records amplifying with at most 2 edits per primer (`max_edits_in_silico: 2`). The 2,166 bacterial records are therefore nearly all exactly at 2+2 edits. Typical gut bacteria, at 3+ forward-primer edits, are absent, so their reads in real runs end up as "unclassified, ~90% to *Prevotella*" and similar, by the hundred.
2. **Options**, depending on what the bacterial reads are for:
   - **Count only** (enough for a helminth assay): nothing to change in the release; the pipeline reports them as an off-target fraction.
   - **Name them at least to family:** add a prokaryote layer built with a looser in-silico PCR (3–4 edits on the forward primer) or from a 16S database (SILVA) trimmed to the same 515–926 region. Mark it as off-target in `taxonomy.tsv` or the manifest, so its looser threshold doesn't leak into eukaryote resolution.
3. **`primer_fit.tsv`:** the 3′-end mismatch classes (`near_3prime`) are where this shows. A summary of fit class by domain in `summary.txt` would make the bacterial exposure visible per release.

Pipeline side, for information: nf-helminth-amplicon's database mode now finds primers at up to 3 edits (`--db_primer_max_edits`, panel mode stays at 2). These reads are therefore trimmed and counted as bacteria rather than dropped as `no_primer`, and the bacterial share it reports will rise accordingly.

## 4. Smaller notes

- **Resolution tables:** the release has `resolution_illumina_150x150.tsv` only. VESPA-style 2x300 runs (windows 250 and 200) would benefit from a `resolution_illumina_300x300.tsv`, since the pipeline reports each call's resolution from the table matching the read length.
- **Illumina windows:** `manifest.json`'s `illumina_windows` [132, 128] is right for 2x150. The pipeline takes other windows as parameters, so nothing is needed unless 2x300 becomes a supported default.
- **Correction to an earlier remark:** v0.4.0's `taxonomy.tsv` does **not** have a row whose domain is the literal `domain`. That was the header line in a shell count. The nine records with no taxonomy at all are real, and listed in §1a.

Both releases were run through the same pipeline code on all 52 VESPA samples (2x301; windows 250x200, and 132x128 as a 2x150 stand-in). The comparison with the paper is on the 12 human samples only, whose names match the paper's Source Data; the paper's primate sample numbers don't match the BioProject's.

### 5c. Still open after v0.7.0

1. **Human gut commensal protozoa: the main gap against the paper.** *Iodamoeba*, *Endolimax* and *Chilomastix* are in the release (18, 14 and 18 records), but these samples' reads sit 4–28% away from them. So they're reported as "unclassified, NN% to X" rather than named. VESPA named them, probably through its NCBI BLAST fallback, so GenBank should hold closer sequences. Of the 86 candidate sequences in `candidate_local_sequences.fasta`, those nearest these genera are the probes to search with.
2. ***Entamoeba*:** unchanged at 217 records; ~230k reads in 39 samples are still only 93–99% to the nearest record (§2).
3. ***Onchocerca*:** VESPA reports it in three human samples. The only filarioid reads here match *Dirofilaria immitis* `V4_2a33a9a8add1` exactly over 450bp, and the release's single *Onchocerca* record merges *O. volvulus* (a 1,234bp WGS fragment) with *O. cervicalis*. Worth checking whether the *O. volvulus* member covers the whole insert, and whether VESPA's call was a near-neighbour assignment.
4. **Bacterial 16S** (§3): unchanged; still only the borderline 2+2-edit records.
5. **`V4_54f09b637b29`:** see 5a.

### 5d. The 40 primate samples: cohort-level comparison with the paper

The paper's primate sample numbers don't correspond to the BioProject's (e.g. BioProject NHP11 matches the paper's "NHP39" within 1% in every category), so these comparisons are across the whole cohort, not sample by sample. They use v0.7.0, 250x200 windows. A genus counts as present in a sample when it carries at least 0.1% of the sample's reads; the paper's prevalence is counted as distinct genera per sample.

**Group shares, median across the 40 samples (paper vs pipeline):** fungi 48.7% vs 44.5%, protozoa 15.5% vs 34.2%, plants + invertebrates 5.5% vs 3.5%, bacteria + archaea 5.2% vs 4.2%, host 1.6% vs 1.3%, helminths 0.6% vs 0.0%. The protozoa difference is probably partly categories: VESPA splits "environmental" from "parasitic" protozoa, and the pipeline's protists include free-living ciliates and others.

**Samples (of 40) with each genus:**

|---|---|---|---|---|

**What this asks of the release:**
1. ***Physaloptera* and primate spirurids:** see 5e. The signal is real but low; closer primate-spirurid references would let it be named once it clears the floor.
2. **Trichostome ciliates** (*Balantidium*, *Neobalantidium*, *Buxtonella*): only 2 *Balantidium* records. These are common primate gut ciliates.
3. **The commensal amoebae and flagellates** (*Endolimax*, *Iodamoeba*, *Enteromonas*, *Chilomastix*): the same gap as in the human samples, larger in primates. Primate-host isolates are probably needed, not just human ones.
4. ***Trichinella nelsoni***: checked in 5e. The record is genuine and the match exact, so this is a finding, not a mislabel.
5. **Primate *Entamoeba*** (*E. chattoni*, *E. polecki* subtypes, *E. nuttalli*): restating §2; it matters more in primates.

These samples were not run with the pipeline's chimera check, so a few of the unclassified sequences above may be chimeras.

### 5e. A closer look at the *Trichinella* and "*Howardula*" clusters (primate samples)

**The *Trichinella* sequence is genuine *Trichinella* 18S.**
- *Where:* the most abundant sequence (~3,100 pairs in 16 samples) matches `V4_97eac3c4dd50` exactly over the 2x301 windows. That record is a single PR2 member, `AY851261`, "*Trichinella nelsoni* 18S ribosomal RNA gene, complete sequence" (1,826 bp, 2005).
- *Nothing else is close:* the nearest other genera are *Trichuris* at 83% and the capillariids (*Capillaria*, *Eucoleus*, *Pearsonema*, *Aonchotheca*, *Calodium*) at 77-78%.
- *Raw reads:* exact fragments of the release's *Trichinella* records occur in 33 of the 40 primate samples (~5,400 reads).
- *Conclusion:* not a mislabel on the release's side or the pipeline's, and the paper's "*Capillaria*" (3 samples) can't be this sequence.

For the release: V4 may not separate *Trichinella* species, and this genus has one record for *T. nelsoni* and two for *T. spiralis*. The `resolution_*` tables should say whether "*nelsoni*" is resolvable over V4, and the release should carry more *Trichinella* species if possible. Trichinellosis in primates is plausible, but the finding needs a check outside this pipeline before anyone relies on it.

**The "nearest *Howardula*" cluster is not *Physaloptera*.**
- *Where:* ~2,600 pairs in 4 samples, 96.4% to `V4_b2c7b5a4eb94`. That record is a single PR2 member, `AF519233`, "*Howardula* sp. SP-MA" 18S, an insect-parasitic tylenchid.
- *Not spirurid:* it is only 80% to the nearest *Physaloptera* record, and no primate sequence has any spirurid as its best hit.
- *Prevalence doesn't fit either:* 4 samples, against the paper's *Physaloptera* in 31.
- *Probably:* an allantonematid-like nematode from insects in the diet, lacking a closer reference.

**Where the paper's *Physaloptera* is.** Exact fragments of the release's *Physaloptera* records occur in 38 of the 40 samples, but only ~1,350 reads in all, a few tens per sample. That's below the pipeline's 0.1% reporting floor and the size needed to form a sequence of its own. The paper's calls are probably real but low-level. Better primate-spirurid references would let them be named once they clear the floor; they wouldn't change detection.

(The fragment checks use one 24-base probe per record from the R1 window, which related taxa may share, so treat counts as indicative.)

### 5f. PCR chimeras on the VESPA nanopore flow cell (v0.7.0)

The pipeline now flags chimeras de novo and excludes a chimera when its two parents are in different genera, or when both parents are "unclassified". Within-genus joins are kept. On the flow cell (50 wells, v0.7.0):
- 4,781 reads (1.2% of classified) were excluded as chimeras. One well (F1) was over 5%.
- The largest cross-genus joins are *Trichuris* x *Ascaris* (~350 reads). Most of the rest join unplaced gut bacteria, often with each other: 2,754 reads came from joins whose parents were both "unclassified".

For the release:
1. **Screen candidate local sequences for chimeras.** Some of the 86 sequences in `candidate_local_sequences.fasta` came from samples run before the check. Re-derive them with chimeras excluded before adding any.
2. **Chimeras inflate the apparent gap.** A chimeric consensus matches nothing well, so it reads as "unclassified, NN% to X". The unclassified gut-bacterial sequences in particular are partly chimeric, which matters if a prokaryote layer is considered (§3). Only non-chimeric unclassified sequences are evidence of a missing taxon.

### 5g. Fungi in the primate samples (full pipeline run, 52 samples, 2x301, v0.7.0)

**The fungal share is real, and it matches the paper.**
- *Where:* fungi are 37% of all classified pairs (1.0M of 2.68M), and 95% of those are in the primate samples.
- *Against the paper:* the primate mean fungal share is 48% in both the paper and the pipeline (medians 50% vs 45%). The human mean is 8% in both.
- *Highest samples:* NHP3, NHP28, NHP6, NHP25, NHP24, NHP40, NHP11 (96% fungi), NHP15, NHP16 and NHP22 together hold 55% of fungal pairs.
- *What they are:* Eurotiomycetes moulds (*Aspergillus* / *Penicillium*, 45%), Saccharomycetales yeasts (13%), *Phallus* (6%), *Candida mucifera* (5%), *Aspergillus*, a *Rhizopus*-like Mucoromycotina and *Microascus*.
- *Probably:* diet, or mould growing on faeces before collection, rather than infection. This is inferred from the taxa only.

For the release:
1. **Eurotiomycetes is the largest unresolved block in these data.** Of the release's 1,333 Eurotiomycetes records, `resolution_illumina_300x300.tsv` resolves 255 only to the class and 21 only above it. The top fungal call (~447k pairs, 100% identity) stops there. That is V4, not the release. The release could still say in `summary.txt` which large clades V4 can't split below class, so a "Eurotiomycetes" call is read as "a mould, genus unknowable" rather than "badly classified".
2. **PR2's fungal ranks are shifted by about two, and it shows in the output.** For fungi the `class` column holds the phylum (Ascomycota), `order` the subphylum (Pezizomycotina) and `family` the class (Eurotiomycetes). A call reported "at family: Eurotiomycetes" is really at class. Relative abundance is unaffected, but per-rank summaries ("reads named to family or better") overstate how deep fungi are named, and the family identity floor is applied to what is really a class. Options: say so in the release notes, or add a `rank_note` column for clades whose columns don't hold the named rank. A remap is possible but would break comparison with PR2.
3. **No action on the fungi themselves.** They classify well, and nothing suggests missing or mislabelled records.

**Pipeline side, for information:** nf-amphid-pipeline's database mode now flags PCR chimeras de novo, excluding joins between two genera or between two unclassified parents (5f). Chimeras are 0.3–1.7% of classified pairs in three VESPA human samples. A chimeric consensus could otherwise look like a gap in the database, so a few of the "unclassified" sequences in `candidate_local_sequences.fasta` may be chimeras, especially ones with low support. Re-derive candidates with chimeras excluded before adding any as local sequences.
